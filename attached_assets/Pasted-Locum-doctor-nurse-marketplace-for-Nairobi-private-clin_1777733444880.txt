Locum doctor/nurse marketplace for Nairobi private clinics
You are building a full, production-grade two-sided marketplace for the Kenyan private healthcare staffing industry. Before you write a single line of code, read this entire brief, think deeply about the problem, expand massively on the vision with the best ideas you can generate that the client hasn't mentioned, then present a comprehensive phased plan of approximately 20 substantial phases. Each phase should be meaty and meaningful — not "set up the project." Only after the client says "next" do you begin executing phase one.
The pain:
Private clinics in Nairobi — from single-doctor practices in Westlands to multi-specialty facilities in Karen and Upperhill — run on skeleton staffing. When a doctor calls in sick on a Saturday, when a clinic wins a new specialist contract and needs cover, when a locum is needed for a maternity leave — the clinic owner gets on the phone and starts calling people they know. It is entirely word-of-mouth. There is no directory, no verified database, no booking system, no contract, no payment trail. Medical staffing agencies exist but charge 25–35% placement fees, are slow, and are designed for permanent placements. The locum economy — short-term, shift-based, fill-in medical work — is completely undigitised in Kenya. Nurses, clinical officers, anaesthetists, radiographers, and GPs want locum work but have no clean way to signal availability or get found. Clinics want reliable cover but have no trusted pool to draw from. Both sides are frustrated.
What you are building:
A two-sided platform — clinics on one side, verified medical professionals on the other. Clinics post shift needs with specialty, date, time, location, and rate. Verified locums see open shifts matching their specialty and availability, apply or are auto-matched, confirm, show up, and get paid through the platform. The platform handles credential verification, contracts (auto-generated per shift), payments (Mpesa for individuals, invoice for clinics), ratings on both sides, and a growing verified talent pool. Think of it as the intersection of Toptal's vetting rigour, Uber's shift-matching speed, and a proper Kenyan compliance layer on top.
Critical nuances you must understand:
Medical credential verification in Kenya means checking Kenya Medical Practitioners and Dentists Council (KMPDC) registration, nursing council registration, and valid practicing certificates — these have public lookup pages that can be checked programmatically or via a structured manual review workflow. The platform must never allow an unverified professional to accept a shift. Clinics must also be verified — not just anyone can post a shift. Rates vary significantly by specialty — a locum GP earns differently from a locum anaesthetist — and the platform should have suggested rate ranges per specialty to set market expectations. Shift disputes (no-show by locum, clinic cancels last minute) need a clear resolution workflow with penalties. Payments to individual locums should be Mpesa. Payments from corporates/clinics should support both Mpesa and bank transfer with auto-generated invoices. The platform should eventually support recurring arrangements — a clinic that uses the same locum every Saturday should be able to formalise that as a standing booking.
Expand massively on this. Think about: a clinic reputation score so locums can avoid bad payers; a shift insurance micro-product; integration with medical council public registers; a locum "availability calendar" that syncs with their primary employer schedule; bulk shift posting for larger facilities; a "clinic network" feature where a group of clinics shares a vetted locum pool; analytics for clinic HR managers on spend, fill rate, and specialty gaps; a mobile-first experience for locums who will primarily use phones; WhatsApp notifications for shift alerts; a locum earnings dashboard with tax summary for KRA returns; and anything else you think would make this the definitive platform for Kenyan medical staffing.

The frontend should be Next.js deployable on Vercel with edge functions where appropriate — for example, real-time shift availability and matching logic should be edge-optimised. The entire backend should be containerised with Docker so it can move to a VPS without any rearchitecting. Provision proper environment variable management, a seed script for demo data, and a one-command local dev setup. All database migrations should be versioned.
